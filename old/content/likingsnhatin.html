3. The UNIX Philosophy and simple coding philosophies:
I obviously like these philophies mainly because they are really non-bullshitty
and simple. I love complexity only when its required, i prefer simplicity
nonetheless. Take the UNIX philosophy for example-
"Make each program do one thing well." - Such common sense, but damn
alot of software devs dont take this to sense. like, Why do we make
big bolomoths of software that does nothing but increases points of
failures and complexity? Like damn. imagine a multimedia app that can
do a lot of multimedia related operations (beyond viewing, like burning)
and say, you use it to burn a movie onto a DVD while doing smth else
and the video you play(unrelated) has an unsupported coded, and that
triggers a bug that causes the entire thing to crash
Great.
Now you're entire DVD Is ruined, corrupted.
(this is just an example of what i mean.)
So, This entire thing wont happen if the purpose was more decentralized
like a seperate media player, a seperate disk burning utility.
even if both are under the same project.

And then take "Avoid captive user interfaces"
which, in simple english- just means, dont make shit that needs a specific
app to work with (Unless absolutely needed) or signing up for basic functionality
this alone would fix the amount of enshittified software we have right now.
